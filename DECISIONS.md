# Accepted decisions

- Two players. The third was dropped once the game was settled as two-player, which also reduced laser identity to telling a steady dot from a blinking one rather than separating three blink periods. ESP32 DevKit V1, classic ESP32/WROOM, 30-pin USB-C boards. All lasers are red. Two buttons per controller.
- Controller power inventory now includes a 9 V-input converter with 5 V and 3.3 V outputs, plus IRLZ44N MOSFETs. The wiring guide uses its 5 V output and leaves 3.3 V unused. Converter current capacity, exact board power input, laser driver type, and servo current remain to be verified on the bench.
- Current bench controller was rewired: D14/GPIO14 is the steady-laser button, D27/GPIO27 is the pulsing-laser button, and D25/GPIO25 drives the laser gate. Requested standalone firmware uses hold-to-run, 2 Hz pulses with 50% duty, pulse priority when both are held, and off when released. The user subsequently requested simultaneous servo jogging on D33: D14 lowers and D27 raises the pulse, 50 us per 20 ms (2× the previous 25 us rate), using the earlier authorized 544–2400 us bounds. Neither/both holds servo position. Wi-Fi remains disabled in this combined bench mode.
- The user confirmed the laser and buttons work with the standalone laser-button test. The servo is labeled T / ANKONGRC micro servo 9g SG90 analog, reported as a 180-degree positional type, powered from the converter's 5 V output. The user confirmed it moved freely on the earlier GPIO26 connection and authorized a 544–2400 microsecond broad travel test. The servo signal has since been reassigned to D33/GPIO33 at the user’s request; operation on this pin remains unverified. Its physical endpoints, available torque, and water mechanism are not yet calibrated. Converter voltage under servo load remains unmeasured.
- Canoes always move forward and steer toward their laser. Near the aim point, or when tracking is ambiguous, retain heading. Ejected beavers are slower and hold fire for thrust.
- Button 1 fires/thrusts; button 2 activates a power-up. First hit ejects the beaver, second eliminates it. Last survivor wins a round; first to five wins the match. Brief invulnerability prevents one attack consuming both states.
- All requested destructibles, asteroids, barrels, turrets, death beams, laser weapons, jousters, and proximity mines stay in scope. Physical wall contact causes no damage.
- Three primary shots; the third starts a configurable complete-magazine recharge. No new weapon activation during recharge. Shot rate and timings are configurable.
- Hands, shadows and props are detected by predicting what the camera should see from the frame being projected (`Vision.set_projection`), measuring the projector's per-channel strength at calibration from its white field against its black markers, and flagging regions materially darker than that prediction. Absolute darkness and local contrast both fail here: a hand is wider than the ink kernel and sits about nine camera levels below the board on the strongest channel, while pastel artwork moves individual channels by up to 148. Handing over the canvas is optional and the worker falls back to drawn ink without it. This also removes the constraint on dark projected art, since artwork is now predicted away rather than outshone.
- Calibration projects nine markers on a three by three grid and needs only `MARKER_MINIMUM` of them, spanning at least `MARKER_SPREAD` of each axis. Requiring all four corner markers made any ink, object or glare over one corner fatal, which the bench hit immediately. Each marker gives four corners of a known square so three suffice, while the span requirement guards the extrapolation error that a reprojection check on the markers cannot see. Markers are only projected during calibration, so the board is unrestricted the rest of the time.
- Ink is read from the red channel alone, because a red laser dot raises red and no mark that lowers red can be mistaken for one. Green and black fill as solid bodies, blue is solid as a line but too broken to fill, and red, orange and pink are undetectable by design; orange measures a negative red drop, reflecting more red than the whiteboard. This is a physical constraint shared with the laser input, not a tuning choice. Projected artwork is undone by `under_white` before ink is read, so the art palette is unconstrained whenever the canvas is handed over.
- Drawings and physical objects update continuously. Reserve dark markings for physical ink/flat props. Hands/shadows can be sensed as temporary barriers; arbitrary raised objects cannot be reconstructed in 3D.
- Physical ink is found by local contrast (a black-hat transform over `camera.wall_stroke`, with `camera.wall_contrast` as the test) rather than by an absolute cutoff alone, which cannot work on a projected surface: measured glare moves the bare board between 158 and 212 while black marker photographs near 150. The absolute `camera.wall_threshold` is retained as an OR for dark areas wider than the kernel. Because the test is local, projected art must avoid strong dark outlines rather than merely a dark average; the current palette clears it by roughly 2.5x on this projector, and `wall_update_hz = 0` keeps only the calibration scan if brighter projection changes that.
- A successful calibration scans the board from its own frame and publishes walls immediately, so a drawing is solid the moment calibration ends. That frame is the cleanest read available because the projector is showing a flat white field; the four markers are excluded by position.
- Physical ink remains solid until erased. Projected destructible objects are separate so damage survives camera updates.
- The two lasers must be matched for brightness in hardware; this is not a threshold that can be tuned around. Measured back to back on one calibration, controller 1's dot reads median area 98 px2 and redness 31, peaking at 53, and is found on 78 percent of the frames its gate is lit. Controller 2's reads median area 1 px2 and redness 18, peaking at 25, and is found on about 11 percent. With both lit the camera resolves both dots on 14 percent of frames, which is far too low for two-player aiming. Redness is bounded by the surface, and the bare board peaks at 15 under a white projection against a threshold of 16, so there is one level of margin and no room to lower it: a threshold that caught controller 2's dot would admit the board. The firmware is identical on both, driving GPIO25 with digitalWrite and no PWM, so the difference is the module or its supply. Swapping modules between controllers separates the two.
- Continuous red-dot tracking plus periodic one-controller-at-a-time identification windows. Do not infer player identity from unsynchronized high-frequency PWM. Ambiguous aim retains last heading until confidently reacquired.
- The laptop running the game is now macOS, so capture selects AVFoundation by default and V4L2 only on Linux; `camera.backend` overrides it. `camera.device` is an index rather than a path, and on this bench the Arducam is index 0 while the built-in camera and an iPhone Continuity camera also enumerate. macOS additionally requires Camera permission for the terminal application, which an app running under App Translocation from `~/Downloads` cannot hold.
- Calibration markers are detected through CLAHE and accumulated across frames for `camera.marker_memory_seconds`, because this projector on a lit whiteboard yields only about twenty gray levels between its own black and white. The reprojection limit still rejects corners mixed from different poses, so this does not weaken the mapping guarantee. Room lighting, not software, is the binding constraint on marker contrast.
- Arducam-1080P-HDR is a USB UVC camera, previously /dev/video0 on Linux; use configurable MJPEG 1280x720 at 30 fps. A 60-frame live sample measured 29.95 fps at 720p. It also advertises 1080p/30 and manual controls; 1080p performance remains unverified.
- HY300PRO at configurable 10.31.171.250; built-in Miracast currently says waiting for connection. The user chose HDMI after wireless casting produced no usable picture; HDMI is now the primary setup path. Preserve laptop MIT internet connectivity. AirScreen's bundled version is blocked by an update prompt.
- The ESP-NOW receiver's serial link runs at 460800 and its radio callback never touches serial. Two controllers at 50 Hz offer 86 percent of a 115200 link, and a blocking `Serial.write` inside the receive callback stalls the Wi-Fi task and makes the driver drop frames, measured as 6.5 percent loss on one controller and none on the other. The callback now fills a ring that `loop` drains, and the heartbeat reports a `dropped` count. 460800 rather than 921600 because this CH340 cable corrupts lines at the higher rate; 460800 keeps four times the headroom with framing intact. `beaver_battle.relay --baud` must match the sketch's `BAUD`.
- Hit feedback is a servo-actuated water mechanism inside each controller. Implement configurable press/return commands and duplicate protection now. User is handling power, cooling, and mechanical construction later; do not treat those as blockers to software delivery.
- One-time setup may use the laptop. Lobby, ready, pause/resume, calibration, and match restart must be possible from controllers.
- Root plus three workers implement in parallel. Bench assembly remains human work. Push runnable milestones to the user-configured origin.

## Shared Python contracts

Coordinates are logical projector pixels, origin at top-left, x right and y down. Walls are a bool NumPy array of shape (height, width), where True is solid. Freshness uses laptop monotonic seconds.

- `PlayerInput` and `FeedbackEvent` are defined in `beaver_battle/model.py`.
- `Game(config)` receives the entire configuration dictionary. `new_match(player_ids, walls=None)` resets a match. `update(dt, inputs, walls=None)` returns a list of FeedbackEvent. `draw(surface)` renders bright placeholder art and HUD. Public `phase` is playing, round_over, or match_over; public `scores` maps IDs to scores. Root handles lobby, calibration, and pause before calling update.
- `Vision(config)` receives the entire configuration. `start()` starts its worker; `stop()` releases the camera. `snapshot()` returns the latest VisionSnapshot. `set_identity(player_id, ready_since)` sets the sole illuminated controller or None; ready_since is the host time after acknowledgements and settling. `begin_calibration()` requests ArUco calibration from the projected marker screen. `draw_calibration(surface)` draws that marker screen. Camera failures are reported in the snapshot rather than terminating the app.
- VisionSnapshot contains timestamp, aims, confidence, walls, preview, calibrated, and error. Treat published arrays as immutable. No queued backlog of frames.
- Game owns its collision and geometry helpers. Vision owns calibration and detection helpers. Both add standalone assert-based checks runnable without hardware.

## Astro Party rule alignment (2026-09-19)

- Emily compared the game with Astro Party (wiki Gameplay and Powerups pages; no source publishes timings) and asked for its rules. Now the defaults: rocks return one at a time (`reload_mode = "each"`, `reload_each = 1.0`), a swimmer who survives `canoe_return = 7.0` s gets a new canoe with `return_invulnerability = 1.5` s, a canoe running over a swimmer sinks it (`ram_swimmers`), and the point goes to whoever does the sinking (`scoring = "kills"`), with the match decided at the end of a round and a shared lead playing on. The earlier decisions above (whole-magazine recharge, no return, last survivor scores) remain available through the same settings, and `checks/check_game.py` pins them; `checks/check_rules.py` covers the new set. Still unlike Astro Party: mines spare their owner, the jouster is timed, asteroids do not split.
